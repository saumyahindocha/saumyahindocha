## Why it exists

I wanted a small, dedicated music player that feels like a modern iPod: stream from my iPhone over Bluetooth, play my own MP3s from an SD card, and show the album art on a proper screen instead of a two-line LCD.

The core requirement was Bluetooth A2DP, so the phone sends audio and Pod plays it through a hi-fi DAC into headphones. That one requirement shaped the first big decision.

## How it works

- **Controller:** Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W. I first planned an ESP32-S3, then dropped it because it has no Bluetooth Classic, which A2DP needs.
- **Audio:** a PCM5102 I2S DAC on `GP13` (DIN), `GP14` (BCK) and `GP15` (WSEL). WSEL must be BCK + 1 because the PIO side-set pins are consecutive.
- **Screen:** a 2.8" 240×320 ILI9341 display with XPT2046 resistive touch and a microSD slot, all sharing SPI0. The display's SDO line stays disconnected so it does not corrupt touch readings.
- **Battery:** a divider on `GP28` read by the firmware, with an optional MAX17048 fuel gauge. A battery icon shows the level and a "Battery low" warning appears below 15 %.

The main firmware is C on the Pico SDK with BTstack. A home screen picks the source:

- **Phone:** the iPhone pairs with "Pod". The firmware adds software volume synced with the phone, sample-rate handling for 44.1 and 48 kHz, and album art downloaded over AVRCP Cover Art.
- **SD card:** MP3 and WAV playback with a touch library browser, tags and cover art, drag-to-seek and auto-advance through a folder.

The "Poster" Now Playing screen draws on the second core, so core 0 keeps Bluetooth and audio. Every push runs a GitHub Actions build that publishes the `.uf2` files, which I flash by drag-and-drop. I write much of this firmware with AI coding agents and review it; the hardware choices, wiring and testing are mine.

The screen on the model at the top of this page runs the Pod's own display code, compiled for the browser. Tap "Use screen" and it behaves like the device: pick Phone or SD card, open a folder, play a track, drag the seek bar, tap the art for the volume slider. The library, songs and album art are made-up placeholders.

## The build

The story so far, from the dated build log:

1. **Breadboard (26 Sep to 7 Oct 2026).** I wired the display, touch and DAC on a breadboard and brought each part up with small Arduino sketches. A 440 Hz tone confirmed the I2S wiring. Then the iPhone paired with "Pod" and streamed Spotify through the DAC to headphones, which met the core requirement.
2. **Firmware on PC (7 Oct).** The home screen, SD player and battery gauge were built and tested on a PC first. The track reader was checked against CBR and VBR MP3, ID3v2.3/2.4 tags and 16/24-bit WAV, and a PC preview renders the screens.
3. **PCB Rev A (7 to 8 Oct).** The first plan was a bare RP2350A with a separate radio on a 4-layer board. I switched to soldering my Pico 2 W onto the board instead, which removed the chip, flash, crystal and regulator design and allowed 2 layers. I drew it in EasyEDA Pro: four schematic sheets, a 52 × 109 mm board, GND pours on both layers and a 9 × 14 mm antenna cut-out. DRC was clean apart from the expected notch item, and I exported the Gerbers, a 23-line BOM and pick-and-place.
4. **Perfboard (8 to 10 Oct).** The JLCPCB quote for the assembled board was about $90, too much for one prototype, so the PCB design stays in the repo and I built the same layout on perfboard. I soldered it in one night, and the firmware now boots to the home screen with touch working, powered over USB.
5. **Case (14 Oct).** I designed a printed case in CadQuery, fully parametric, with every dimension a named value at the top of the script. Rev A was a 57 × 114 × 23 mm snap-fit tray and bezel with no external screws. The same day, Rev B replaced the snap-fit with four M2 screws from underneath into pegs on the bezel, countersunk and hidden under the rubber feet. It came out at 57.2 × 125.0 × 21.8 mm: 5.4 mm longer at each end for the screw blocks and 0.9 mm thinner. The cavities are sized from the parts I ordered: TP4056 charger 29 × 17.3 × 4.3 mm, slide switch 13 × 6.5 × 5.5 mm, a 403048 LiPo and the GY-PCM5102 DAC. The repo has the STL and STEP files and a PDF with 1:1 paper templates. It has not been printed yet.

Still to do: fit the replacement DAC and test audio again, then the TP4056 charger, switch and battery, then paper-check the screen window with the 1:1 template and print the case. Album art over Bluetooth now works on the real hardware.

## What broke and how I fixed it

- **Swapped pins (26 Sep, 7 Oct).** Display RESET and touch T_DO were swapped on the breadboard. Later, MOSI sat on `GP21`, which is not a valid SPI0 data pin, so I moved it.
- **Touch needed a hard press (7 Oct).** The touch library has a fixed pressure cutoff of 300. I wrote `PodTouch`, a driver with an adjustable threshold (60 after testing), extra averaging and calibration built in.
- **Compiler kept breaking (7 Oct).** The laptop's antivirus removed parts of the toolchain, so all compiling moved to GitHub Actions.
- **Schematic review (7 Oct).** On the power sheet, charger pins were unconnected, the two 22 µF output capacitors were in series and the gauge's 1 µF capacitor was missing.
- **Gerber outline (8 Oct).** A stray 6.35 mm line from the jack footprint sat 2.2 mm inside the bottom edge. The fab would have milled it as a slot, so I removed it from the outline file.
- **Dead DAC (10 Oct).** A wiring mistake on the perfboard killed the Adafruit PCM5102 DAC. A generic GY-PCM5102 board is on order to replace it; on that board the SCK pin goes to ground.
- **Touch read nothing (10 Oct).** On the finished perfboard, the pressed signal worked but every reading was all 0 or all 8191, and the setup dots accepted the garbage. Printing the raw readings on the screen pointed to the data line: the link from `GP4` to T_DO (holes F8 to F10) was open. Resoldering it fixed touch. I also hardened the firmware: MISO pulled up, readings above 4095 rejected as "line open", and a new calibration magic so the old breadboard calibration is ignored.

## What I took away

Pod taught me as much about debugging and planning as about audio.
