## Why it exists

SS Filters sells side-stream filtration for industrial cooling water. Their pitch is simple: fouling makes a cooling tower reject less heat, so the fans, pumps and chiller burn more power, and that power has a price. During my internship in July 2026 I built a dashboard that makes that price visible, tower by tower, in rupees.

The target is a factory, not the cloud. Each tower gets its own field devices (a flow meter, RTDs, pressure sensors, a filter DP sensor, conductivity, turbidity, pH, ambient temperature and humidity, and three power meters). They report over Modbus RTU to a PLC, and an IoT gateway bridges that to MQTT on the factory LAN.

The live demo is linked at the end of this page. It is hosted on Render and may take about 30 s to wake up on the first visit.

## How it works

The backend is a single FastAPI service with three background loops:

- **Ingestion.** In the demo, an in-process simulator generates readings for two towers every 15 s, with fouling that slowly drifts upward. With `MQTT_ENABLED` set, it instead subscribes to `factory/towers/+/telemetry`. There is also a `POST /api/ingest` route for gateways that only speak HTTP. Every reading is stored with its `tower_id`, and each device's `last_seen` is updated.
- **Analytics.** Every 60 s in dev, for each tower:
  - Heat rejection `Q = ṁ·Cp·ΔT`, with `Cp = 4.186 kJ/kg·K`.
  - Tower effectiveness, (return − supply) / (return − wet bulb).
  - Specific power in kW/TR, with 1 TR = 3.517 kW.
  - A fouling index from 0 to 1, a weighted sum of filter DP, conductivity, turbidity and pressure drop across the tower, graded Clean, Watch, Fouled or Critical at 0.35, 0.6 and 0.85.
  - Hours to cleaning, from a linear fit of the last 6 h of fouling index.
  - Excess power against a per-tower baseline line (slope × TR load + intercept), multiplied by the tariff to give rupees lost per interval and a running total.
- **Staleness and alerts.** A device that has not reported for 90 s raises an offline alert. Fouling breaches and excess power above 25 kW raise alerts too, deduplicated over 5 minutes.

The React frontend has a multi-tower overview, a per-tower page with sensor tiles, power tiles, a power-vs-time chart with the baseline overlaid, a fouling panel and the rupee counter, plus an alerts table and a tariff dialog. A Process page uses three.js to show the full heat path, from server racks through the chiller and condenser to the cooling tower, and lists the audit kit.

## The build

The repo was generated with Emergent, an AI app-building platform. Its fingerprints are clear: an `.emergent/` folder, a `test_result.md` protocol for its main and testing agents, and an automated smoke-test report in `test_reports/`. That report records every backend endpoint group and every frontend page passing, including the tariff dialog changing the top bar to ₹11.25/kWh.

The production path is written down but not yet proven on a real plant. `docker-compose.yml` brings up Mosquitto, TimescaleDB, MongoDB, the backend, the Nginx-served frontend and an optional MQTT publisher simulator. A TimescaleDB schema with hypertables for readings and analytics is included. No real PLC has been wired to it yet.

The top-level `README.md` is still a placeholder ("Here are your Instructions"), and `frontend/README.md` is the default Create React App file. The best description of the system lives in `memory/PRD.md` and `on-prem/README.md`.

## What broke and how I fixed it

The repo has a single commit, dated 9 July 2026, so there is no build log to quote. What the code does record:

- **Platform-locked login.** The generated app used Emergent's hosted Google sign-in, which only works inside their environment. The commit removes the auth wrappers from the frontend and opens the dashboard routes. The backend still carries the old `/api/auth/*` routes, and `frontend/src/lib/auth.jsx` is still in the tree but unused. Removing both is open work.
- **Two data stores.** The app runs on MongoDB, while the on-prem plan targets TimescaleDB. The schema is there, but the backend does not write to it yet. A Mongo-to-Timescale adapter is listed as a P1 backlog item.
- **A hosted logo.** The SS Filters logo is loaded from Emergent's asset CDN, not from the repo. That has to move into the repo before an offline factory install.

Still in the backlog: CSV export, SMS and email alerts, a baseline re-fit tool, a side-by-side tower comparison view, and role-based auth for the LAN.
